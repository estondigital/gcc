# A research-first pre-launch strategy

Growth Clarity Co.

A practical planning framework, not a platform launch timetable.

## 1. Start with a learning objective

Define the decision your research should inform: whose problem to prioritise, which workflow to understand, or what evidence a useful product must connect. Interest in an idea and evidence of a recurring problem are different signals.

## 2. Invite the right conversations

Describe the role, company context and challenge you want to learn about. Keep the research invitation specific and low-pressure: a short conversation about current practice, without a demo or a sales pitch.

## 3. Give each visitor a clear path

Research participants need an interview invitation. Buyers with a current need need a separate consulting enquiry. People interested in product feedback need a platform-interest path that makes development status and selection limits explicit.

## 4. Make the landing page useful

Explain who the conversation is for, what it covers, how long it takes and what happens next. Offer a practical resource so visitors can understand your thinking before deciding to participate.

## 5. Qualify without overloading the form

Collect the role, company context, growth challenge and willingness to talk. Keep optional product-update consent separate from research contact consent. Never require confidential business data for an introductory conversation.

## 6. Connect the invitation to the interview

Use campaign source information to understand which invitations reach relevant participants. Review responses manually, confirm fit, agree a conversation time and keep a record of the research stage. A form submission is not a completed interview.

## 7. Turn observations into evidence

Ask for a recent example, the steps the team took, the tools and people involved, and the decision they made. Separate what the participant did from what they say they might do with a future product.

## 8. Review the quality of learning

Track qualified interest, conversations completed, recurring problems and unresolved questions. Look for patterns across comparable participants; do not present a small research sample as a market-wide benchmark.

## 9. Move towards product feedback deliberately

Invite further feedback when a problem and use case align with the product direction. State expectations, participation effort and current product status. An expression of interest should never imply guaranteed access.
