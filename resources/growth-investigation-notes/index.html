Growth Investigation Notes

Turn an ambiguous symptom into a question, an evidence requirement and a practical next investigation. These are illustrative frameworks, not validated diagnoses or research findings.

02
Acquisition & qualification

More traffic. The same pipeline.

Are we attracting more of the right buyers?

Start with an observation

Write down the traffic change and the pipeline change separately. Name the period, source and target segment. A rise in visits does not establish that more potential customers reached your site.

Gather the evidence

Compare source-level visits, relevant enquiries, qualified opportunities and sales acceptance. Check that qualification rules and attribution have stayed consistent. Allow for the time between an enquiry and an opportunity.

Test competing explanations

An audience shift, lower buying intent, a weak landing-page promise or a delayed sales handoff could each explain the same pattern. Review customer fit and intent before increasing spend or rewriting the entire funnel.

Choose a practical next step

Review a small set of recent enquiries with sales. Record why each was accepted or rejected, then compare the patterns by source. Use that evidence to choose one audience, message or handoff experiment.

Bring a decision to the meeting

Which source deserves a closer investigation, what evidence would change our view, and who will review the result? Avoid treating a correlation as the root cause.

03
Sales conversion

More demos. Fewer wins.

Did buyer quality change, or did the buying journey?

Define comparable cohorts

Compare opportunities with enough time to reach a decision. A recent campaign can inflate open pipeline while making an immature cohort look less successful.

Gather the evidence

Review qualification notes, customer segment, demo source, opportunity age, buying committee and loss reasons. Use conversation notes to understand objections that a CRM stage alone cannot explain.

Test competing explanations

The product may be reaching a new segment, the message may attract buyers with different expectations, or the sales process may miss a stakeholder. Separate these possibilities from simple lead-volume changes.

Choose a practical next step

Compare a few won and lost opportunities from the same segment and period. Map where expectations diverged and identify one qualification or enablement question to test.

Bring a decision to the meeting

Should the next investigation focus on who books, what they expect, or how the decision is supported? State the remaining uncertainty.

04
Product activation

Signups without the first moment of value.

Where does the customer stop making meaningful progress?

Define value before the metric

Agree on an activation event that represents useful customer progress. Completing a checklist or logging in is not automatically evidence that the customer experienced value.

Gather the evidence

Map onboarding steps, time to the value event and abandonment by persona, acquisition source and intended use case. Check instrumentation and speak with people who stalled.

Test competing explanations

Wrong-fit signups, missing data, setup dependencies, unclear expectations and product friction may all contribute. An aggregated funnel can hide these different experiences.

Choose a practical next step

Trace one activated and one inactive journey within the same persona. Identify the first meaningful divergence and test a small improvement with clear observation criteria.

Bring a decision to the meeting

Which onboarding obstacle is supported by both behavioural and customer evidence? Define what success would look like without assuming a guaranteed retention effect.

05
Customer value & retention

Acquisition is growing. Retention is weakening.

Does the promise made at acquisition match the value delivered?

Separate the signals

Distinguish customer retention, revenue retention and repeat product use. Compare cohorts at the same age before concluding that the overall customer experience has deteriorated.

Gather the evidence

Connect acquisition source and original use case with onboarding, adoption, support themes, renewal conversations and churn reasons. Note where evidence is missing rather than filling gaps with assumptions.

Test competing explanations

New customer segments may need different onboarding. Acquisition messaging may overstate fit. Customers may not adopt the workflow required to see value. Each explanation calls for a different investigation.

Choose a practical next step

Follow one weak-retention cohort from its first promise through onboarding and renewal. Ask where expectations and actual use began to diverge.

Bring a decision to the meeting

Should the team change targeting, onboarding or ongoing adoption support first? Identify an owner and a checkpoint for learning.

06
Growth economics

Revenue rises. Acquisition efficiency slips.

Which part of the growth motion is becoming more expensive?

Use aligned definitions

Keep the cost period, customer cohort and revenue basis consistent. Document what acquisition costs include and how gross margin and sales-cycle timing affect the comparison.

Gather the evidence

Review fully loaded acquisition costs, new-customer revenue, gross margin, sales-cycle length and expansion by segment. Check allocation changes before interpreting a headline ratio.

Test competing explanations

Channel saturation, a more complex buying process, a shift in contract size or higher delivery costs can worsen efficiency while revenue still grows. Do not assume one explanation from an average.

Choose a practical next step

Reconstruct one cohort calculation and identify the largest change in its inputs. Investigate that input before recommending a broad budget cut or channel expansion.

Bring a decision to the meeting

Which assumption needs validation before the team reallocates resources? Record the trade-off and evidence needed for the next decision.
